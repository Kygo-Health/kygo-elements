Read this before you trust a number

The one thing that decides accuracy.

Who's wearing it matters more than which brand it is. The same device scores far higher in screened healthy sleepers than in older adults or real sleep-clinic patients, which is why the comparison above splits staging into healthy vs clinical.

Screened healthy adults

κ 0.65 best case

Ceiling numbers live here

Young, health-screened sleepers. The manufacturer-funded "best" figures come from this population: a best-case sample, not a method trick.

Older adults & patients

κ 0.30–0.42 real users

Every device drops

Sleep-clinic patients and older adults. Apnea-fragmented sleep is harder to stage, so Oura falls 0.65 → 0.35 and Apple 0.53 → 0.30. Fitbit is the exception (0.42 in both).

A 4-stage kappa of 0.4–0.6 is only "moderate," the current ceiling for consumer devices. Two human PSG scorers agree at ~0.75, so no wearable is "accurate" in an absolute sense. Schyvens 2025 · Lee 2023

True for every device

What no brand can fix.

Five findings that hold across every wearable in the tables. Read these before you trust any single number.

Universal

No device reliably measures wake

Every wearable has high sleep sensitivity (89–95%) but low wake specificity (27–52%). They all overestimate sleep and underestimate time awake (WASO) by 12–48 min. No wrist or ring reliably tells you how long you were actually awake.

Schyvens 2025 · Haghayegh 2019

Age effect

Accuracy drops sharply with age

In older adults, total sleep time is underestimated by roughly 75 min and deep-sleep error explodes (MAPE 400–700%) on both the Fitbit Sense 2 and the Oura Gen 3. Numbers from young cohorts do not generalize.

Searles 2026

Better tier

EEG headbands beat every ring & watch

Muse-S and ear-EEG headbands reach kappa ~0.76, above any consumer ring or watch and matching human scorers. If you need genuine staging accuracy, that is the class to look at, not a wrist device.

npj Biomedical Innovations 2025 meta

Form factor

Rings win vitals, watches win stages

Rings (Oura) lead the vitals measured during sleep: HRV (CCC 0.99), resting HR (CCC 0.98), temperature. Watches (Apple) tend to win the stage labels. Both share the same wake-detection weakness.

Dial 2025

Coverage gap

The newest models are unvalidated

Every independent staging study tested a prior generation. Oura Ring 4/5, WHOOP 5/MG, Apple S9/S10/Ultra, Galaxy Watch/Ring and Pixel carry manufacturer-only claims, because peer review lags hardware by 18–36 months.

Validation coverage gap, 2026

The bottom line

Oura leads sleep staging (κ 0.65 healthy, its own funded study) and total sleep time (bias about 3 min). Apple is the most accurate in independent testing (κ 0.53) and best for REM in healthy sleepers. Fitbit is the most population-robust (κ 0.42 healthy and clinical). WHOOP wins independent-healthy deep sleep, while Garmin and Withings trail on staging and over-report sleep the most. But every device overestimates sleep, can't reliably measure wake, and collapses in older adults and clinical patients, so use the trend, not the absolute number.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which wearable is the most accurate for sleep?

Oura leads on sleep staging (Cohen's kappa 0.65 in healthy adults, from its own funded study) and on total sleep time (bias about 3 minutes). Apple is the most accurate in independent testing (kappa 0.53). But no consumer device is "accurate" in an absolute sense: the best land at moderate agreement (kappa around 0.5), while two human sleep scorers agree at about 0.75.

Are sleep trackers accurate for sleep stages?

Only moderately. A 4-stage agreement of kappa 0.4 to 0.6 is the current ceiling for consumer wearables. Every device overestimates sleep, misses wake, and drops sharply in older adults and in people with sleep disorders. Use the trend over weeks, not a single night's stage breakdown.

Why do the accuracy numbers drop for people with sleep problems?

It is a population effect, not a study disagreement. Screened healthy sleepers score far higher than real sleep-clinic patients. Oura falls from kappa 0.65 (healthy) to 0.35 (clinical); Apple from 0.53 to 0.30. Apnea-fragmented sleep is simply harder to stage, so every device drops. Fitbit is the most robust, holding kappa 0.42 in both.

Can my watch or ring tell how long I was awake?

Not reliably. Every device has high sleep sensitivity (89 to 95%) but low wake specificity (27 to 52%), so they all overestimate sleep and underestimate time awake by 12 to 48 minutes. Wake detection is the universal weakness across every brand.

Do rings or watches track sleep better?

They win different things. Rings like Oura win the vitals measured during sleep (heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and temperature) plus total sleep time. Watches like Apple tend to win the actual stage labels (light, deep, REM). Both share the wake-detection weakness.

Are the newest models like Oura Ring 4, WHOOP 5 and Apple Series 10 more accurate?

Unknown independently. Every published validation study tested a prior generation, so all current-generation accuracy claims are manufacturer-only. Peer-reviewed validation lags new hardware by roughly 18 to 36 months, so newer does not yet mean proven.
