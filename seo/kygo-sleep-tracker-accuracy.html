The one thing that decides accuracy.

Who's wearing it matters more than which brand it is. The same device scores far higher in screened healthy sleepers than in older adults or real sleep-clinic patients, which is why the comparison above splits staging into healthy vs clinical.

True for every device

What no brand can fix.

Five findings that hold across every wearable in the tables. Read these before you trust any single number.

Universal

No device reliably measures wake

Every wearable catches most of your sleep but misses a large share of wake: from 27% of wake caught (Garmin) to 73% (Oura) in healthy adults. Most overestimate sleep and underestimate time awake at night, by up to 48 min. No wrist or ring reliably tells you how long you were actually awake.

Robbins 2024 · Svensson 2024 · Miller 2022

Age effect

Accuracy drops sharply with age

In older adults, total sleep time is underestimated by roughly 75 min and deep-sleep error explodes (MAPE 400–700%) on both the Fitbit Sense 2 and the Oura Gen 3. Numbers from young cohorts do not generalize.

Searles 2026

Better tier

EEG headbands beat every ring & watch

Muse-S and ear-EEG headbands reach kappa ~0.76, above any consumer ring or watch and matching human scorers. If you need genuine staging accuracy, that is the class to look at, not a wrist device.

npj Biomedical Innovations 2025 meta

Form factor

The ring leads vitals and stages

Oura leads the vitals measured during sleep: HRV (CCC 0.99), resting HR (CCC 0.98), temperature. It also leads 4-stage staging (kappa 0.65 vs Apple Watch 0.60 in the same study). Apple Watch's edge is REM detection (82.6%). Both share the same wake-detection weakness.

Dial 2025 · Robbins 2024

Coverage gap

The newest models are unvalidated

Every independent staging study tested a prior generation. Oura Ring 4/5, WHOOP 5/MG, Apple S9/S10/Ultra, Galaxy Watch/Ring and Pixel carry manufacturer-only claims, because peer review lags hardware by 18–36 months.

Validation coverage gap, 2026

The bottom line

Oura leads sleep staging (κ 0.65 in healthy adults, Oura-funded Robbins 2024) and total sleep time (about 2 min off). Apple Watch is second (κ 0.60) with the best REM detection (82.6%), and Fitbit third (κ 0.55) while holding up best in sleep-clinic patients (0.42). WHOOP (0.44), Polar (0.28) and Garmin (0.25) trail on staging, and Garmin over-reports sleep the most (+43.8 min). But every device overestimates sleep, can't reliably measure wake, and collapses in older adults and clinical patients, so use the trend, not the absolute number.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which wearable is the most accurate for sleep?

Oura leads on sleep staging: Cohen's kappa 0.65 in healthy adults, ahead of Apple Watch at 0.60 and Fitbit at 0.55 in the same in-lab study (Robbins 2024, Oura-funded). Oura is also closest on total sleep time (about 2 minutes off). WHOOP (0.44), Polar (0.28) and Garmin (0.25) trail. No consumer device is "accurate" in an absolute sense: two human sleep scorers agree at about 0.75.

Are sleep trackers accurate for sleep stages?

Only moderately. In healthy adults the best consumer wearables reach a 4-stage kappa of about 0.55 to 0.65, against about 0.75 between two human scorers. Every device overestimates sleep, misses wake, and drops sharply in older adults and in people with sleep disorders. Use the trend over weeks, not a single night's stage breakdown.

Why do the accuracy numbers drop for people with sleep problems?

It is a population effect, not a study disagreement. Screened healthy sleepers score far higher than real sleep-clinic patients. Oura falls from kappa 0.65 (healthy) to 0.35 (clinical); Apple from 0.60 to 0.30. Apnea-fragmented sleep is simply harder to stage, so every device drops. Fitbit holds up best, from 0.55 to 0.42.

Can my watch or ring tell how long I was awake?

Not reliably. Every device catches most of your sleep but misses a large share of wake: from 27% of wake caught (Garmin) to 73% (Oura) in healthy adults. Most overestimate sleep and underestimate time awake at night, by up to 48 minutes. Wake detection is the universal weakness across every brand.

Do rings or watches track sleep better?

In the studies so far, the ring leads. Oura leads the vitals measured during sleep (heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and temperature), 4-stage staging (kappa 0.65 vs Apple Watch 0.60 in the same study) and total sleep time. Apple Watch has the best REM detection (82.6%). Both share the wake-detection weakness.

Are the newest models like Oura Ring 4, WHOOP 5 and Apple Series 10 more accurate?

Unknown independently. Every published validation study tested a prior generation, so all current-generation accuracy claims are manufacturer-only. Peer-reviewed validation lags new hardware by roughly 18 to 36 months, so newer does not yet mean proven.
