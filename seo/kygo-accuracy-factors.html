Tattoos

Not a small drift: over ink the sensor often returns nothing at all.

HR

SpO2

Matters

36% dropped out

The number

The study
n=25, tattooed skin against clear skin on the same arm, optical sensor against a chest ECG. Resting error 22.9% over ink against 2.9% on clear skin, and 9 of 25 people had total dropout. Ink darkness and tattoo age did not predict failure. Navalta and Bunn 2025.
What the brands say
Apple says "some tattoos, can also impact heart rate sensor performance." Garmin says ink "can block the light from the sensor." Polar says to avoid placing the sensor on them. Samsung says to wear its ring on a finger without tattoos. Google is silent.
What to do
Move the sensor to clear skin: higher up the forearm, the other wrist, or an armband.

Higher body fat

Three of four watches ran well high on calories, and the error grew as body fat rises. Skin tone made no difference.

Calories

Matters for calories

+69 kcal in 10 min

The number

The study
n=58 Hispanic adults, a 10-minute cycling test against a COSMED K5 metabolic system. Average overestimate: Garmin Forerunner 955 +69 kcal, Samsung Galaxy Watch 5 +57, Apple Watch Series 8 +22, Fitbit Sense 2 +3 but with 13% of its readings implausible (over 4.5 times the true value). Error rose with body fat on every brand, p<0.01; skin tone had no significant effect. One short cycling protocol, so treat the size as indicative. Kostrna 2026.
What the brands say
No manufacturer addresses body composition in its calorie accuracy guidance.
What to do
Read workout calories as a high estimate, and more so the higher your body fat. Keep your weight current in the app, and track the trend rather than eating back the number.

Cold hands

Cold cuts the optical signal roughly in half, and the device drops readings rather than guessing.

HR

HRV

SpO2

Matters

Signal -41%

The number

The study
Ice over the forearm cut the raw optical signal by 41%, n=21. Warming the wrist for 15 minutes took blood-oxygen error from 4.1 points to zero, independent of skin tone, n=46. In a 10 C chamber the average looked fine while the ability to track change collapsed, with one ring going from 0.78 to 0.32 on concordance.
What the brands say
Apple says cold exercise means "skin perfusion in your wrist might be too low." Google says "cold weather can hinder your device’s ability to accurately measure your heart rate during exercise." Samsung says "keep yourself warm." Oura says "cold fingers can cause signal problems." Garmin says to warm up for 5 to 10 minutes first.
What to do
Warm up before you trust a workout reading, and expect gaps on cold nights and winter runs.

Too tight or too loose

Loose fails at every intensity. Too tight loses part of the pulse wave. The right pressure is personal.

HR

HRV

Matters

23 to 47% better

The number

The study
A custom wrist rig with a load cell, n=17: tuning the pressure per person beat a universal setting by 23 to 47%, and loose (12 mmHg) failed at every intensity. A second rig, n=27, showed that excess pressure flattens the waveform. No study has tested a consumer strap at graded notches.
What the brands say
Apple says "tighten your Apple Watch band for workouts, then loosen it a bit when you’re finished." Google says "snug but not constricting, as a tight band restricts blood flow." Polar says tighten for training and loosen after. Fitbit Air says a pinky finger should slide under the band.
What to do
One notch tighter for workouts, back off for sleep.

Hairy arms

The one study that graded arm hair found no difference in accuracy. Shaving has never been tested on its own.

HR

Does not matter

p = 0.29

The number

The study
Cardiac rehab patients, n=30, four-point photographic hair scale, Fitbit against a chest ECG. Hair density did not differ between the patients whose readings were accurate and the patients whose readings were not. Shaving was bundled with cleaning the sensor and taping the watch down, and that bundle helped 3 of 10. Vermunicht 2025.
What the brands say
Samsung lists "body hair, dirt, or other objects" as obstructions. WHOOP says hairy arms are marked safe. Apple, Garmin, Google, Oura and Polar do not mention hair at all.
What to do
Nothing. If your readings are poor, fix position and tightness first, because shaving is unproven.

Skin tone

Average error is the same across skin tones. The spread is wider, and the effect shows up as missing readings rather than wrong ones.

HR

SpO2

Does not matter on average

Bias: no difference

The number

The study
Null in the best-powered studies: n=53 with a balanced Fitzpatrick sample, and n=28 measured with an objective colorimeter rather than a self-report scale. Pooled bias was null in every stratum, but the limits of agreement were 2.2 times wider in dark skin, and dark-skin participants supplied a disproportionate share of the missing data for 2 of the 3 devices tested. Calories too: skin tone had no significant effect on calorie error across four watches, n=58 (Kostrna 2026), while body fat did.
What the brands say
No manufacturer addresses skin tone on a wear page.
What to do
Check completeness, not just the average. Gaps in the graph are the symptom here, not a shifted number.

Common questions

Wearable accuracy, answered honestly.

What is the single biggest thing I can do to make my wearable more accurate?

Move it higher up the forearm and keep it snug during exercise. In the one study that tested position, three finger widths above the wrist bone cut movement error from 20.5% to 7.3%. Ten people, so a strong lead rather than a law, but every manufacturer’s own guidance points the same way.

Do hairy arms affect a wearable?

In the only study that graded arm hair, hair density made no difference to accuracy. Shaving was never tested on its own. Samsung lists hair as an obstruction; WHOOP says hairy arms are fine.

Do tattoos stop a wearable reading your heart rate?

Often, yes: 36% of people in the one study had the sensor return nothing over ink at rest. Ink darkness and tattoo age did not predict it. Move the sensor to clear skin.

Does skin tone affect wearable heart rate accuracy?

Not the average error, in the best-powered studies. The spread is wider and the effect appears as missing readings rather than wrong ones.

Does body fat affect smartwatch calorie accuracy?

Yes. Against a metabolic system during cycling, n=58, every watch tested overestimated calories and the error grew with body fat on every brand. Skin tone made no significant difference.

Does cold weather make my wearable less accurate?

Cold roughly halves the optical signal and devices drop readings. Warm up first; expect gaps in winter.

Does it matter which wrist I wear it on?

Not for heart rate or sleep. For steps, the dominant wrist logs about 1,250 more a day, so pick one and set it in the app.

Are smart rings more accurate than watches?

At night, slightly, for resting heart rate. By day under movement a ring placed 9th of 10 devices. A ring that rotates loses most of its signal, so fit matters more than form factor.

Can I trust the calories from a weights session?

No. Heart rate tracks fine, but energy expenditure read 116% high against a metabolic cart. Halve it.
