Apple Watch

25.95% max treadmill test (Ultra 2), 3rd of four brands head to head

How it calculates

Proprietary ML neural networks trained on metabolic-chamber studies. Combines heart rate, motion, GPS speed, elevation and your profile. Active + Resting calories are summed for the total.

  • Sensors Optical HR (green-LED PPG), 3-axis accelerometer, gyroscope, GPS, barometric altimeter.
  • Resting rate Resting rate likely from the Harris-Benedict equation (Apple does not disclose it).
  • Best measured Walking (~20%)
  • Worst measured Strength (~112.2%)
  • Tendency Reads high in the newest head-to-head studies: about 14% high on endurance and 112% high on weights (Lee 2026). Sex is not a tested moderator.

Strengths

  • Most-studied device, a 56-study meta-analysis (Choe & Kang 2025)
  • Reasonable for steady-state cardio and walking
  • Heart rate itself is strong: no subgroup in that meta-analysis exceeded the 10% error threshold*

Watch-outs

  • Weight training 112.2% off (Series 8, Yun 2026, n=70), and it reads high: +112% in Lee 2026
  • Cycling 35.7% off, reading 21.6 kcal high (Series 8, Kostrna 2026, n=58)
  • The often-quoted ~28% from the meta-analysis traces to a press release and is pooled bout-level, not a daily total: the paper's own pooled figure is 0.30 kcal/min (limits -2.09 to 2.69)

Fitbit

13.97% max treadmill test (Charge 6), 1st of four brands head to head

How it calculates

Heart rate is the primary driver: each minute in an HR zone becomes METs, then calories using your weight. Steps only matter indirectly by raising HR.

  • Sensors 3-axis accelerometer, optical HR, altimeter, gyroscope; Charge 6 adds ECG, SpO2, EDA and skin temperature.
  • Resting rate A standard metabolic equation (likely Mifflin-St Jeor, undisclosed).
  • Best measured Running (12 to 15%)
  • Worst measured Walking (~69%)
  • Tendency Near-zero average bias, but wide individual swing (-5 to +6 kcal/min). Chevance's own framing: 3 kcal/min over an hour is about 180 kcal, roughly a 40% miss.

Strengths

  • Lowest error of four brands in the newest head-to-head studies: 13.97% on a max treadmill test (Charge 6), 31.7% cycling (Sense 2) and 41.7% weight training (Charge 5)
  • Closest of four devices on weights in Lee 2026: 145.95 kcal vs a 140.79 kcal criterion, about 4% high
  • Bias near zero on all comparisons in a 52-study meta-analysis (k=49: 0.19 kcal/min, SD 2.53, limits -5.32 to 5.70)

Watch-outs

  • Walking overestimated by 69% (Charge 2, O'Driscoll 2020, n=59)
  • The 31.7% cycling figure holds only after 7 implausible readings were removed; with them the Sense 2 read 128.6 kcal high (Kostrna 2026)
  • Remove the low-quality studies from that meta-analysis and the same table gives k=29: -2.77 kcal/min, limits -12.75 to 7.41

Garmin

14.65% max treadmill test (Forerunner 265S), unplaced on calories

How it calculates

Firstbeat engine: beat-by-beat R-R interval analysis derives respiration rate from HRV, estimates VO2, then converts to METs → calories. Adding respiration lifts accuracy over HR-only methods.

  • Sensors PPG optical HR (Elevate Gen 4 red/IR; Gen 5 adds green LEDs), accelerometer, GPS, ANT+/BLE chest-strap compatible.
  • Resting rate A standard metabolic equation (undisclosed); nudged up for light daily movement.
  • Best measured Running (~22%)
  • Worst measured Strength (~114.9%)
  • Tendency Reads high in the newest studies: +30.5% on endurance and +116.4% on weights (Lee 2026). A 2020 lab review on older hardware found underestimation.

Strengths

  • Most sophisticated engine (Firstbeat HRV modeling)
  • Treadmill running holds up: 14.65% on a max treadmill test (Forerunner 265S, Perez 2025) and 19.1% in de Leon 2026 (Vivoactive 4, n=18, unfunded)
  • A paired chest strap sharply improves accuracy

Watch-outs

  • Cycling 77.6% off, reading 68.6 kcal high (Forerunner 955, Kostrna 2026)
  • Weight training 114.9% off (Vivosmart 5, Yun 2026, n=70), and Lee 2026 found it reading +116.4% high on weights
  • Fuller 2020 (lab only, 51 non-independent comparisons on 2013 to 2019 hardware, one author employed by Garmin during publication) found underestimation, the opposite of the newer studies

WHOOP

No study no WHOOP calorie validation has been published

How it calculates

ACSM metabolic equations extended by Keytel et al. 2005: Calories = BMR + f(heart rate), activating once HR rises above your resting baseline. Recovery status shifts the estimate.

  • Sensors Advanced PPG (MAX86171 on 4.0), accelerometer, skin temperature, SpO2; 5.0 adds ~26 Hz sampling + respiratory rate.
  • Resting rate Age, sex, height, weight; 5.0 adds a 30-day personalized calibration window.
  • Tendency Unknown. With no calorie study there is no measured direction. The estimate is recovery-coupled, so the same workout can read differently by day.

Strengths

  • Built for relative strain and recovery trends
  • The HR and HRV signal itself is well-validated by Bellenger et al. 2021, which validated HR and HRV only, never calories
  • 5.0 adds a 30-day personalized calibration window

Watch-outs

  • No calorie study of any WHOOP model has been published, so this tool publishes no WHOOP calorie figure
  • Calorie percentages that circulate online for WHOOP trace to no primary publication (no authors, DOI or PubMed record)
  • No GPS and no screen, so workouts rely on heart rate alone

Oura Ring

21.1% lab average of 8 activities (Ring Gen 2, 2023), not ranked

How it calculates

BMR + activity calories via METs across the day (resets at 4 AM). A Nov 2024 update folded in HR intensity, cutting median active-calorie error by 53%. Recognizes 40+ activity types.

  • Sensors 18-path multi-wavelength PPG (red/IR/green LEDs), 3D accelerometer, 2 precision thermistors.
  • Resting rate A standard metabolic equation using age, sex, height and weight.
  • Tendency Underestimates as effort rises: 0.4 MET low on average in the one lab test, and 3.5 MET low when running at 15 km/h (Ring Gen 2, Kristiansson 2023).

Strengths

  • Built for daily activity and recovery trends
  • Finger PPG gives a cleaner resting signal than the wrist
  • A Nov 2024 update folded heart rate into activity calories (an Oura figure, not a study)

Watch-outs

  • 21.1% off against lab gas analysis on average, with the miss growing as running speed rose (Ring Gen 2, Kristiansson 2023)
  • Its only daily-total comparison used a wrist accelerometer as the reference, not lab gas analysis, so this tool does not use it
  • No GPS; poor for cycling/elliptical (no hand motion)

Samsung Galaxy Watch

10 to 13% running (Galaxy Watch 6, 7)†, 2nd of four brands head to head

How it calculates

HR-VO2 relationships combined with accelerometer and GPS; MET tables applied to recognized activities. Watch 7 adds a BIA body-composition sensor whose role in the calorie model is undocumented.

  • Sensors Optical HR (PPG), 3-axis accelerometer, gyroscope, barometer, GPS; Watch 7 adds the BioActive sensor (ECG, BIA body composition).
  • Resting rate A standard metabolic equation (undisclosed, Harris-Benedict or Mifflin-St Jeor).
  • Best measured Running (10 to 13%)
  • Worst measured Strength (~84.5%)
  • Tendency Close on running and walking, but reads high on cycling and weights (+83% on weights, Lee 2026).

Strengths

  • Lowest error of five brands on a max treadmill test: 9.42% (Galaxy Watch 7, Perez 2025, conference abstract)
  • Running 10 to 13% (Galaxy Watch 6, 7, Ferreira 2026, n=148)†
  • Walking within 0.5% of the criterion total (Galaxy Watch 5, Oh 2024, 27 men, 30-minute walk)

Watch-outs

  • Cycling 66.5% off, reading 56.8 kcal high (Galaxy Watch 5, Kostrna 2026)
  • Weight training 84.5% off (Galaxy Watch 5, Yun 2026, n=70), reading 83% high in Lee 2026
  • † The running study was Samsung-funded and all its authors work at Samsung partner Sidia

Hidden variables

Factors that skew every device.

Even the best wearable can't see these, and each one shifts the calorie estimate.

Body composition / BMI

High impact

Algorithms are trained on average builds. Very lean, muscular or higher-BMI bodies skew the HR-to-calorie conversion, and Fitbit is specifically less accurate at higher BMI.

Medications (beta-blockers, stimulants)

High impact

Beta-blockers cap heart rate and cause big underestimation; stimulants raise resting HR and inflate the count. Devices can't detect either.

Tattoos

High impact

Dense or dark ink over the sensor blocks light transmission and can make wrist PPG readings unreliable.

Device fit & placement

High impact

Loose bands, wrist hair and low placement all add motion artifact. The band should sit snug, 1 to 2 finger-widths above the wrist bone.

Skin tone

Moderate

LED optical sensors read darker pigmentation less consistently. Garmin Elevate Gen 5 and Apple Series 9+ improved this, but a Series 9 study still found tracking inconsistent across pigmentation groups.

Sex

Moderate

Shcherbina et al. 2017 found sex a significant predictor of heart rate error across seven devices, with error rates for men running about 4% higher than for women. That is heart rate, not calories, and it is not specific to any one brand. No calorie meta-analysis has tested sex as a moderator.

Age

Moderate

Optical signal quality drops with skin changes, and the 220−age max-HR assumption drifts further off with age.

Caffeine & hormonal state

Moderate

Caffeine lifts heart rate independent of effort; menstrual cycle, thyroid and other hormonal shifts move metabolic rate in ways no wearable can see.

The bottom line

Wearables get heart rate right (~96%) but the leap from heart rate to calories carries 15 to 40%+ real-world error, up to 93% in the Stanford study. Steady cardio is the safe zone; strength training and cycling are the worst regardless of brand, and pooled across the Stanford devices the error was actually highest at rest (52.4%), not during exercise. Use the number for week-over-week trends and relative effort, never as an exact figure to eat back.

FAQ

Common questions.

How accurate is Apple Watch calorie burn?

Apple Watch placed third of four brands head to head: 25.95% off on a max treadmill test (Ultra 2, Perez 2025, conference abstract), 35.7% on cycling (Series 8, Kostrna 2026) and 112.2% on weight training (Series 8, Yun 2026), where it reads high. It reads best for walking (~20%) and running (~24%) in Le 2022. The often-quoted ~28% from a 56-study meta-analysis (Choe and Kang 2025) traces to a press release and is pooled across bouts; the paper's own figure is 0.30 kcal/min (limits -2.09 to 2.69).

Which wearable is most accurate for calories?

Fitbit, then Samsung, then Apple Watch, but no brand came within 9% on the max treadmill test. In head-to-head lab studies, Fitbit was 13.97% off on a max treadmill test (Charge 6), 31.7% on cycling (Sense 2) and 41.7% on weights (Charge 5). Samsung was 9.42% on the treadmill (Galaxy Watch 7) but 66.5% on cycling and 84.5% on weights (Galaxy Watch 5). Apple Watch was 25.95%, 35.7% and 112.2%; Garmin 14.65%, 77.6% and 114.9%. WHOOP has no calorie study, and Oura's one lab test (Ring Gen 2) was 21.1% off averaged over eight activities, a different protocol that is not ranked. Fuller 2020 found no brand within 3% of the criterion more than 13% of the time. In the Stanford seven-device study (Shcherbina 2017), calorie error was lowest for walking (31.8%) and running (31.0%) and highest for sitting (52.4%), so the worst errors happen at rest.

Why does my wearable overestimate or underestimate calories?

Wearables do not measure calories directly. They infer them from heart rate, motion and your profile, so the conversion carries the error. Sex, skin tone, body composition, medications (beta-blockers, stimulants), tattoos and band fit all shift the result. Static, low-motion work like strength training is the hardest case for every brand.

Is the calorie number on my watch good enough for a diet?

Not for precise nutrition math. Real-world calorie error runs 15 to 40%+, and the Stanford 2017 study found some devices off by up to 93%. Use the number for week-over-week trends and relative effort, not as an exact figure to eat back.

How accurate is Oura, WHOOP or Garmin for exercise calories?

Oura has one lab test against gas analysis: Ring Gen 2 was 21.1% off averaged over eight activities from sitting to running (Kristiansson 2023, n=28), reading lower as running speed rose. It was measured in METs on a different protocol, so it is not ranked against the head-to-head studies. No calorie study of any WHOOP model has been published; Bellenger 2021 validated WHOOP's heart rate and HRV only, never its calories. Garmin is close on treadmill running, 14.65% on a max treadmill test (Forerunner 265S, Perez 2025) and 19.1% in de Leon 2026 (Vivoactive 4, n=18), but reads high on cycling (77.6%, Forerunner 955, Kostrna 2026) and weight training (114.9%, Vivosmart 5, Yun 2026).
