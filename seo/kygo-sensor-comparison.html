Why the Hardware Differences Matter

Same metric name doesn't mean the same measurement. Here's how hardware design affects what you actually get.

Heart Rate & HRV

Oura's 18-path PPG on the finger reads through thinner skin with less motion noise: 31% fewer nighttime gaps vs Gen 3. Whoop counters with 26 Hz sampling (fastest here) for better exercise tracking. Garmin's Elevate Gen 5 added red + IR LEDs alongside green for better accuracy across skin tones.

SpO2 (Blood Oxygen)

All six use red + IR light, but Oura's finger placement and 18-path design gets 120% better signal quality than Gen 3. Whoop runs continuous overnight SpO2. Everyone else does periodic or on-demand checks.

Sleep Apnea

Only Apple Watch (S10, Ultra 3). Uses the accelerometer, not SpO2: detects tiny wrist movements from interrupted breathing. Needs 30 nights of data. 89% accurate for severe apnea, 43% for moderate. FDA-authorized.

Stress Measurement

Fitbit is the only device with a dedicated stress sensor (EDA) that reads sweat gland activity via skin conductance, a direct nervous system signal. Requires 90-sec manual scan. Garmin, Whoop, and Oura estimate stress from HRV (indirect). Apple has no stress feature.

Hypertension

Only Apple Watch Ultra 3. Analyzes how blood vessels respond to heartbeats via PPG. Does NOT give BP numbers; flags 30-day trends. Algorithm trained on 100,000+ participants.

Fall Detection

Garmin and both Apple Watches use accelerometer + gyroscope; the gyro adds rotational data to tell real falls from arm drops. Whoop and Oura lack gyroscopes so can't do this.

Common questions

Which wearable has the most sensors?

Apple Watch Ultra 3 has the most sensors at 11, followed by Garmin Venu 4 and Apple Watch S10 at 10 each. Whoop 5.0 and Oura Ring 4 have 4 sensors each.

Which wearable has the most FDA-cleared features?

Apple Watch Ultra 3 leads with 3 FDA-cleared features (ECG/AFib, Sleep Apnea, Hypertension). Apple Watch S10 has 2 (ECG, Apnea). Garmin and Fitbit each have 1 (ECG/AFib). Whoop and Oura have none.

What is the difference between hardware and software features on wearables?

Hardware features are physical sensors (PPG, ECG, accelerometer, EDA) that collect raw biometric data. Software features are proprietary algorithms (Body Battery, Recovery Score, Readiness Score) that process sensor data into actionable health insights. The same sensor can produce very different results depending on the algorithm.

Which wearable is best for stress tracking?

Fitbit Charge 6 is the only device with a dedicated EDA (electrodermal activity) stress sensor that measures sweat gland activity, a direct nervous system signal. Garmin, Whoop, and Oura estimate stress indirectly from HRV. Apple Watch has no stress feature.

Which wearable has the best heart rate sensor?

It depends on when heart rate is measured. In independent studies, Apple Watch is the most accurate during exercise, followed by Garmin and Fitbit. Oura Ring 4 is the most accurate overnight at rest, for resting heart rate and HRV, reading from an 18-path PPG sensor on the finger. Whoop 5.0 has the fastest sampling rate at 26 Hz.
