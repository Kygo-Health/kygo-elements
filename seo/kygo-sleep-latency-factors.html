Best Single Habit

Warm bath 1–2 h before bed

Key stat: About −10 min

Bathing at 40 to 42.5°C for as little as 10 minutes, 1 to 2 hours before bed, cut time to fall asleep by about 10 minutes across pooled studies.

Category: Environment & Sleep Hygiene

Best for Insomnia

CBT-I

Key stat: −19 min, 20 RCTs

Structured insomnia therapy cut time to fall asleep by about 19 minutes across 20 RCTs. A 2 to 4 session behavioral version (BBTI) works about as well.

Category: Therapies & Routines

Best Supplement

Melatonin

Key stat: −7 min, 19 RCTs

About 7 minutes faster across 19 RCTs. The effect peaks around 4 mg, taken about 3 hours before bed; more isn't better.

Category: Supplements

Best Exercise

Regular exercise

Key stat: d=0.35, 66 studies

A 66-study meta-analysis found regular exercise shortens sleep onset. In people with insomnia, yoga and Tai Chi cut diary sleep onset by about 25 to 29 minutes (low certainty).

Category: Exercise & Movement

Quickest Impact

Write tomorrow's to-do list

Key stat: About −9 min, one night

Five minutes writing a specific to-do list at bedtime: people fell asleep in 15.8 vs 25.1 minutes on a lab sleep study.

Category: Therapies & Routines

Biggest Sleep-Onset Killer

Caffeine

Key stat: +9.1 min SOL

Adds about 9 minutes to falling asleep on average across 24 studies, and the effect wasn't explained by dose or timing.

Category: Nutrition & Substances

Slower sleep onset

Older Age

SOL lengthens with age, mostly before 60

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Falling asleep takes longer as you get older, mostly before 60.

Mechanism

Sleep gets lighter with age (more stage 1 and 2 sleep), and the body clock signal weakens. After 60, only sleep efficiency keeps declining.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 65 studies, n=3,577, PSG/actigraphy: SOL, WASO, %N1 and %N2 increase with age in adults; after 60 only sleep efficiency keeps declining. Ohayon 2004.

Source

Ohayon et al., Sleep 2004

Slower sleep onset

Evening Chronotype

SOL 21.1 vs 14.1 min, night owls vs larks

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Night owls take longer to fall asleep at a normal bedtime.

Mechanism

Evening types' melatonin rises 2 to 3 hours later, so a conventional bedtime arrives before their body is ready for sleep.

Dosage / Context

53 evening vs 35 morning types, 7 nights: actigraphy SOL 21.1 vs 14.1 min (p=.02); in-lab PSG (Colelli 2023, n=2,612) also links evening type to longer SOL. Evening types' melatonin onset runs 2 to 3 h later (Lack 2009). Lamprou 2024.

Source

Lamprou et al., Eur J Neurosci 2024

Slower sleep onset

Pre-Sleep Cognitive Arousal

High arousal: 37 min longer to fall asleep (n=52)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Racing thoughts at bedtime make it harder to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Worry and racing thoughts keep the brain alert, working against the wind-down needed to fall asleep. Adding mental load in the lab also lengthened sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Observational, n=52, 2 PSG nights: high vs low pre-sleep cognitive arousal took 37 min longer to fall asleep (Kalmbach 2020). Lab experiment (Wuyts 2011, n=15): induced cognitive load prolonged PSG SOL.

Source

Kalmbach et al., 2020

Melatonin

−7 min to fall asleep (19 RCTs); peaks near 4 mg

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Melatonin cuts time to fall asleep by about 7 minutes. More isn't better past about 4 mg, and timing matters.

Mechanism

Melatonin acts on MT1 and MT2 receptors, signalling biological night to the body clock and promoting sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 19 RCTs, n=1,683, primary sleep disorders: SOL −7.06 min (95% CI 4.37 to 9.75, p<0.001); objective −5.5 min, subjective −10.7 min; longer trials gave larger effects (Ferracioli-Oda 2013). Dose-response meta-analysis, 26 RCTs: SOL and total sleep effects peak around 4 mg/day, taken about 3 h before bed (Cruz-Sanabria 2024).

Source

Ferracioli-Oda et al., PLoS One 2013Cruz-Sanabria et al., J Pineal Res 2024

Probiotics

No effect on measured latency (14 studies)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Probiotics haven't been shown to help you fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

The gut-brain axis is a plausible route, but trials have not shown a measurable effect on sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Ashwagandha

SMD −0.53 for SOL (3 RCTs, actigraphy)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Ashwagandha shortens sleep onset, most clearly in people with insomnia.

Mechanism

Proposed to lower stress hormones and pre-sleep arousal and to act on GABA signalling; the mechanism in humans is not established.

Faster sleep onset

Glycine (3 g)

Faster sleep onset on PSG (n=11, p=0.01)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

3 grams of glycine before bed helps you fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

In animal work, glycine widens skin blood vessels so core temperature falls, part of the body's sleep-onset signal. Not confirmed as the human mechanism.

Dosage / Context

Randomized single-blind crossover, n=11 poor sleepers, PSG: 3 g before bed shortened latency to sleep onset (p=0.01) and to slow-wave sleep (p=0.019). Corresponding author works for Ajinomoto, a glycine maker. Yamadera 2007.

GABA (300 mg)

SOL 13.4 → 5.7 min at 300 mg (n=40, PSG)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

300 mg GABA shortened sleep onset in one small trial; low doses haven't beaten placebo.

Mechanism

Oral GABA barely crosses into the brain, so any effect may run through the gut and vagus nerve; this is not established.

Dosage / Context

A milk peptide shortened sleep onset in one sleep-lab trial but not another.

Mechanism

Lactium contains a milk peptide (alpha-casozepine) that binds GABA-A receptors in animal studies, a proposed calming route.

Saffron may trim a few minutes in older adults, but results are inconsistent.

Caffeine

+9.1 min to fall asleep (24-study meta)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Caffeine delays sleep onset by about 9 minutes on average.

Mechanism

Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, masking the sleep pressure that builds through the day.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 24 studies (19 on SOL, mostly PSG): +9.1 min SOL (95% CI 3.8 to 14.4); not moderated by final dose or timing. Gardiner 2023.

Source

Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2023

Mixed

Alcohol

Faster onset only at about 5+ drinks; REM hit from low doses

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Heavy drinking knocks you out faster but wrecks later sleep.

Mechanism

Alcohol boosts GABA-A signalling, which is sedating at high doses. As it wears off later in the night, sleep fragments and REM is disrupted.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 27 studies, PSG, healthy adults: SOL shortens 6.4 min per 1 g/kg (95% CI 3.5 to 9.3); significant only at about 0.85 g/kg (~5 drinks) or more; REM disrupted from low doses. Gardiner 2025.

Source

Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2025

Faster sleep onset

High-GI Carb Meal

SOL 9.0 vs 17.5 min, high vs low GI (n=12)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

High-GI carbs about 4 hours before bed help you fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Carbohydrate raises blood tryptophan, the building block of serotonin and melatonin. The authors proposed this route; the study did not measure it.

Dosage / Context

Randomized crossover, n=12 healthy men, PSG: SOL 9.0 vs 17.5 min, high vs low GI rice meal 4 h before bed (p=0.009); the same meal 1 h before bed gave 14.6 min. Afaghi 2007.

Source

Afaghi et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2007

Slower sleep onset

Nicotine / Smoking

Smokers +5.4 min to fall asleep (n=6,400)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Smokers take longer to fall asleep. Former smokers were no different from never smokers.

Mechanism

Nicotine is a stimulant acting on nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, and smokers may go into mild withdrawal overnight. Former smokers matching never smokers suggests the effect reverses after quitting.

Dosage / Context

PSG cohort, n=6,400 (Sleep Heart Health Study): current smokers +5.4 min SOL vs never smokers (95% CI 2.9 to 7.9); former smokers no different (Zhang 2006). PSG case-control, 44 smokers vs 44 matched never smokers: longer sleep latency in smokers (Jaehne 2012).

Source

Zhang et al., Am J Epidemiol 2006Jaehne et al., Sleep Med 2012

No clear effect

Cannabis

No consistent effect; quitting lengthens SOL

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Cannabis doesn't reliably help you fall asleep, and quitting makes it harder for a while.

Mechanism

THC acts on CB1 receptors and can be sedating, but tolerance builds with regular use. Stopping after regular use brings withdrawal insomnia.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 18 PSG studies (9 pooled): no consistent effect on SOL; withdrawal from regular use consistently prolongs SOL. Velzeboer 2025.

Source

Velzeboer et al., Sleep Med Rev 2025

Slower sleep onset

Energy Drinks

Daily users ~1.8x more likely to take 2 h+ (n=53,226)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Students who drink energy drinks more often take longer to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Energy drinks are a concentrated caffeine source, and caffeine blocks the adenosine signal that builds sleep pressure. The study was a survey, so it shows a link, not cause.

Dosage / Context

Cross-sectional, n=53,226 Norwegian students, self-report: daily vs never/seldom use, risk of SOL over 2 h RR 1.89 in men (1.62 to 2.21) and 1.77 in women (1.60 to 1.97); risk rises from 1 to 3 times a month upward; age-adjusted only. Kaldenbach 2024.

Source

Kaldenbach et al., BMJ Open 2024

Slower sleep onset

Cocaine Use

Longer SOL a top finding across 19 studies

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Cocaine use makes it take longer to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Cocaine blocks the reuptake of dopamine and noradrenaline, a strongly wake-promoting effect, and sleep is also disrupted during withdrawal.

Dosage / Context

Systematic review, 19 studies (12 human), objective sleep only: longer SOL was one of the four most consistent findings, with lower total sleep, sleep efficiency and REM. Medigue 2025.

Source

Medigue et al., Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2025

Mixed

Alpha-Lactalbumin Drink

5 of 8 studies positive; 2 RCTs null

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

A tryptophan-rich milk protein drink may help you fall asleep faster, but trials disagree.

Mechanism

Alpha-lactalbumin is a whey protein rich in tryptophan, the building block of serotonin and melatonin. Whether one drink raises brain tryptophan enough to matter is not established.

Dosage / Context

Systematic review, 8 studies, 5 positive, SOL the most improved metric (not pooled). RCT, n=18 female rugby players, actigraphy: SOL 11.6 and 17.0 min vs 21.2 and 22.5 min on a whey control (interaction p=0.01). Nulls: Ferguson 2021 (n=15), Barnard 2025 (n=24, 7 nights).

Source

Barnard et al., J Sleep Res 2024Gratwicke et al., Biol Sport 2022

No clear effect

Intermittent Fasting

Mostly null across 16 RCTs

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Fasting diets mostly don't change how fast you fall asleep.

Mechanism

No consistent pathway shown. In single comparisons, alternate-day fasting and time-restricted eating did slightly worse than plain calorie restriction.

Dosage / Context

Bayesian network meta-analysis, 16 RCTs, adults with overweight/obesity: most comparisons null; alternate-day fasting worse PSQI latency score vs calorie restriction (MD 1.04, 0.15 to 1.93); time-restricted eating 9.54 min longer SOL than calorie restriction (3.43 to 15.68). Zhang 2025.

Source

Zhang et al., Nutr Metab Cardiovasc Dis 2025

Regular Exercise

d=0.35 shorter SOL (66-study meta)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Exercising regularly helps you fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Regular activity builds sleep pressure and lowers anxiety, both of which help sleep onset. The benefit shrinks in older samples.

Slow breathing and relaxation lower pre-sleep arousal. The evidence is low certainty and mostly from sleep diaries.

Slower sleep onset

Vigorous Exercise <1 h Before Bed

Possible only when a hard session ends ≤1 h before bed

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Evening workouts are fine; only very hard sessions right before bed may delay sleep.

Mechanism

Hard exercise raises heart rate, core temperature and stress hormones, which take time to settle. Evening exercise overall did not impair sleep.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 23 studies, healthy adults: evening exercise overall did not impair sleep. The authors suggest SOL may suffer only after vigorous exercise ending 1 h or less before bed (not a pooled estimate). Stutz 2019.

Source

−19 min to fall asleep (20 RCTs)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Structured insomnia therapy cuts time to fall asleep by about 19 minutes.

Mechanism

Combines stimulus control, sleep restriction, work on unhelpful sleep worries and sleep education, retraining the bed as a cue for sleep and lowering bedtime arousal.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 20 RCTs, n=1,162 chronic insomnia: diary SOL −19.03 min (95% CI 14.12 to 23.93) vs inactive controls. Trauer 2015.

Source

Trauer et al., Ann Intern Med 2015

−19.8 min SOL vs usual care (18 RCTs)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

A 2 to 4 session behavioral program works about as well as full CBT-I.

Mechanism

A short behavioral version of CBT-I built mainly on stimulus control and sleep restriction.

Dosage / Context

Network meta-analysis, 18 RCTs, n=1,104: SOL −19.81 min (−30.64 to −8.98) vs usual care; face-to-face BBTI similar to CBT-I. Hasan 2025.

Source

−12.6 min diary SOL (96 RCTs); smaller on trackers

Evidence: Strong

Insomnia apps help you feel you fall asleep faster; trackers show a smaller effect.

Mechanism

The same components as CBT-I, delivered by app or website. Self-reported gains are larger than tracker-measured ones.

Dosage / Context

Faster sleep onset

Writing a To-Do List at Bedtime

SOL 15.8 vs 25.1 min (n=57, PSG)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Writing tomorrow's to-do list before bed helped people fall asleep about 9 minutes faster.

Mechanism

Writing down unfinished tasks may offload the worry about them that keeps the mind busy at lights out. The more specific the list, the faster people fell asleep.

Dosage / Context

Randomized experiment, n=57 healthy adults aged 18 to 30, PSG: a 5 min to-do list 15.8 vs 25.1 min after writing about completed tasks (p=0.02, d=0.63). Scullin 2017.

Source

Scullin et al., J Exp Psychol Gen 2017

Mixed

Music at Bedtime

Feels easier; measured SOL may not change (13 RCTs)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Music makes falling asleep feel easier, but trackers don't show a change.

Mechanism

Music may relax and distract from worry; objective measures have not confirmed a faster sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Cochrane review, 13 RCTs, n=1,007 insomnia: self-rated SOL problems score −0.60 (−0.83 to −0.37; 3 studies, low certainty); objective SOL (3 studies, n=136) may not improve. Jespersen 2022.

Source

Jespersen et al., Cochrane 2022

Mixed

Hypnosis, Autogenic Training, Imagery

Beat waitlist, not sham (13 RCTs)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Relaxation scripts may help, but no more than a fake version.

Mechanism

Relaxation scripts aim to lower arousal, but sham versions worked as well, pointing to expectation or general relaxation rather than the technique.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 13 RCTs, n=502, diary SOL: hypnotherapy vs waitlist SMD −0.88 (−1.56 to −0.19); autogenic/imagery −1.16 (−1.92 to −0.40); neither beat sham; 11 of 13 trials low quality. Lam 2015.

Source

Lam et al., Complement Ther Med 2015

No clear effect

Acupuncture

+1.5 min vs sham needling (12 RCTs)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Acupuncture doesn't beat fake needling for falling asleep.

Mechanism

No specific effect beyond sham needling; the apparent benefit came from acupuncture added to medication.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 12 RCTs, PSG SOL −7.25 min (−11.29 to −3.20), driven by acupuncture plus medication vs medication alone; vs sham +1.54 min (−4.98 to 8.06). Hu 2026.

Source

Hu et al., Front Med 2026

Warm Bath/Shower 1–2 h Before Bed

About 10 min faster (13 pooled studies)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

A warm bath before bed helps you fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Warming the skin opens blood vessels in the hands and feet, so heat leaves the body faster afterward, helping the core-temperature drop that comes with sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 17 studies reviewed (13 pooled): bathing at 40 to 42.5°C, 1 to 2 h before bed, for as little as 10 min significantly shortened SOL, by about 10 min on average (authors' university release; pooled minutes not in the abstract). Haghayegh 2019.

Source

Haghayegh et al., Sleep Med Rev 2019

Faster sleep onset

Stimulus Control (Bed = Sleep Only)

g=−0.85 vs passive controls (11 trials)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

Only using bed for sleep trains you to fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Reconditions the bed as a cue for sleep instead of for lying awake and worrying, lowering arousal at bedtime.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 11 trials, insomnia, diary SOL: g=−0.85 vs passive controls (95% CI −1.64 to −0.06, 5 studies; −0.87 at follow-up); vs active treatments −0.22, not significant. Jansson-Fröjmark 2023.

Source

Jansson-Fröjmark et al., J Sleep Res 2023

No clear effect

Daily Screen Time (Youth)

Later bedtime, not longer SOL (25 studies)

Evidence: Strong

Plain English

On heavier screen days kids go to bed later but don't take longer to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Screen-heavy days push the time of sleep later rather than lengthening the time it takes to fall asleep once in bed.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 25 within-person studies, n=4,562 aged 3 to 25: more screen time linked to later sleep onset time (r=0.079), not to longer SOL. Bourke 2026.

Source

Bourke et al., JAMA Pediatr 2026

Slower sleep onset

Bright Evening Light

Longer SOL as melanopic light rises (12 lab studies)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

The more melatonin-suppressing light before bed, the longer it takes to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Light-sensitive (melanopsin) cells in the eye signal the body clock, which suppresses evening melatonin.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 12 lab PSG studies: pooled SOL effect 0.69 not significant (95% CI −0.50 to 1.88), but melanopic light from 100 to 1,000 lux showed a dose-response with longer SOL (r=0.59, p=.027). Cajochen 2022.

Source

Cajochen et al., Light Res Technol 2022

Slower sleep onset

Bedroom Noise

Louder bedroom, longer SOL (n=1,076, sound meter)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Noisier bedrooms are linked to taking longer to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Sound can cause brief arousals and keep the brain more alert, delaying the transition into sleep. The study was observational.

Dosage / Context

Cross-sectional, n=1,076 adults 60+, bedside sound meter + actigraphy: each 1 dB of indoor noise linked to longer SOL (β 0.02 log-min, p<0.001) and +0.66 min WASO. Yamagami 2023.

Source

Yamagami et al., Sleep 2023

Slower sleep onset

Hot Bedroom

+11.3 min on heat-event nights (409 nights)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

A hot bedroom adds a few minutes to falling asleep, and about 10 on a heatwave night; it hurts staying asleep more.

Mechanism

A warm room makes it harder to shed heat through the skin, slowing the core-temperature drop that comes with sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Cohort, n=19 adults, 409 nights, bedroom sensors + actigraphy: +1.85 min SOL per +5°C indoors; heat-event nights +11.3 min (95% CI 2.6 to 20.8) (Caballero-Gomez 2025). Li 2021 (n=68,604, accelerometer): +0.11 min SOL per °C outdoors, vs +0.48 min WASO.

Source

Caballero-Gomez et al., Int J Environ Res Public Health 2025Li et al., 2021, n=68,604

Mixed

Phone Use Before Bed

No devices 2 h before bed: SOL −6 min (n=24)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Phone use right before bed is linked to taking longer to fall asleep, though not every study agrees.

Mechanism

Likely a mix of mental stimulation and screen light; studies disagree on how much it matters.

Dosage / Context

PSG crossover, n=24: banning devices 2 h before bed cut SOL 6.08 min. Wearable EEG, n=21: +0.2 min SOL per minute of pre-bed phone use. Diary crossover, 16 athletes: 36.6 vs 16.8 min. Against: RCT n=167, 1 h phone, actigraphy null. Lu 2025; Montanari 2026.

Source

Lu et al., Behav Sleep Med 2025Montanari et al., JMIR 2026

Slower sleep onset

Light-Emitting eReader vs Book

SOL 25.7 vs 15.8 min after ~4 h tablet reading

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Reading on a glowing tablet for hours before bed added about 10 minutes.

Mechanism

Hours of tablet light suppressed and delayed evening melatonin, shifting the body clock later.

Dosage / Context

Inpatient randomized crossover, n=12, PSG, ~4 h reading for 5 evenings: SOL 25.7 vs 15.8 min (p=0.009). Two shorter tablet studies (1 to 2 h) were null. Chang 2015.

Source

Chang et al., PNAS 2015

Faster sleep onset

Low-Melanopic Screen Light

Shorter latency than a standard screen (72 men)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Screens that emit less body-clock-stimulating light let people fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Melanopsin cells in the eye drive evening melatonin suppression; light with less melanopic content stimulates them less.

Dosage / Context

Lab crossover, 72 men, 4 h evening screen use, PSG: lower melanopic light shortened latency in the brightest group (p=0.024); latency about 50% longer from ~5 to ~150 lux melanopic. Schöllhorn 2023.

Source

Schöllhorn et al., Commun Biol 2023

Mixed

Morning Bright Light Therapy

g=−0.55 measured SOL; another meta found none

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Morning light boxes may help some poor sleepers fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Bright morning light shifts the body clock earlier, so melatonin rises earlier in the evening.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 14 studies, n=444 insomnia (primary or comorbid): objective SOL g=−0.545 (Wasilah 2026). Conflicting meta-analysis (Chambe 2023, 13 studies, light at any time of day): no SOL effect.

Source

Wasilah et al., Int J Nurs Stud 2026

Faster sleep onset

Earplugs + Eye Mask

SOL 46.6 vs 71.4 min in a noisy, bright room

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Earplugs and an eye mask helped people fall asleep faster in a noisy, bright room.

Mechanism

Blocking noise and light removes the arousals they cause.

Dosage / Context

RCT, healthy adults in simulated ICU noise and light (n=10 per arm), PSG: SOL 46.6 vs 71.4 min with no aid (p=0.010). Huang 2015.

Source

Huang et al., Crit Care 2015

Faster sleep onset

Skin Warming / Bed Socks

Gentle trunk-skin warming: SOL −3.1 min (26%)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Gently warming the skin, like bed socks in a cool room, helps you fall asleep a few minutes sooner.

Mechanism

Slightly warmer skin on the trunk, within the normal nightly range, sped sleep onset; the authors suggest skin temperature feeds into the brain's sleep centres. In that study, changing core or hand-and-foot temperature alone did not.

Dosage / Context

Lab, n=8, 144 trials: +0.78°C proximal skin warming shortened SOL 3.09 min (1.91 to 4.28), 26% (Raymann 2005). Crossover, n=6 men, 23°C room, actigraphy: bed socks 7.5 min shorter (Ko 2018). Warm footbath studies null.

Source

Raymann et al., Am J Physiol 2005Ko & Lee, J Physiol Anthropol 2018

Faster sleep onset

Gentle Bed Rocking

Faster sleep onset on a rocking bed (n=18)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Sleeping on a slowly rocking bed helped people fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Slow rhythmic rocking stimulates the inner-ear balance system and synchronized sleep brain rhythms (slow oscillations and spindles) in this study.

Dosage / Context

Crossover, n=18, full-night rocking, PSG: shorter latency to NREM sleep, more deep sleep, fewer arousals. Perrault 2019.

Source

Perrault et al., Curr Biol 2019

Slower sleep onset

Bedroom CO2

SOL rose linearly from 800 to 3,000 ppm (n=12)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Stuffier bedroom air was linked to taking longer to fall asleep.

Mechanism

Not established. Higher CO2 is also a marker of poor ventilation, so the effect may come from the air quality overall.

Dosage / Context

Chamber study, n=12, 800 / 1,900 / 3,000 ppm over 54 days: SOL rose linearly with CO2. Xu 2020.

Source

Xu et al., Indoor Air 2020

Faster sleep onset

Sleep-Tech Wearables

−4.5 min measured SOL (5 studies)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Some sleep-tech wearables trim a few minutes off falling asleep.

Mechanism

The devices differ (neurostimulation, neurofeedback, light glasses), so there is no single pathway.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 16 RCTs, n=910 insomnia (neurostimulation, neurofeedback, light glasses): objective SOL −4.52 min (−8.38 to −0.67), 5 studies; subjective null. Zhu 2026.

Source

Zhu et al., JMIR 2026

No clear effect

Weighted Blankets

No significant change in 3 crossover RCTs

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Weighted blankets haven't been shown to help people fall asleep faster.

Mechanism

Deep pressure is proposed to be calming, but trials have not shown faster sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

3 crossover RCTs: ADHD children n=94 (actigraphy, p=0.43), adopted children n=30, adults with depression n=40 (−4.2 min, p=0.06, maker co-funded). Lönn 2023.

Source

Lönn et al., J Sleep Res 2023

No clear effect

Cooling Bedding

No SOL difference (9 RCTs)

Evidence: Moderate

Plain English

Cooling mattresses and pillows haven't been shown to speed sleep onset.

Mechanism

Cooling surfaces target body temperature, but trials in healthy adults found no effect on sleep onset.

Dosage / Context

Meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, healthy adults: no SOL difference; very low to low certainty. Pasquier 2025.

Source

Pasquier et al., J Therm Biol 2025
