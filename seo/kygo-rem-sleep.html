What actually influences your REM sleep?

REM is the stage tied to dreaming, emotional processing, and memory — and it's unusually sensitive to chemistry and body temperature. We dug into the research on what really moves dream sleep, ranked by evidence strength. Here's what helps, what hurts, and what's just hype.

Lowers REM

Lowers REM

The supplement literature for REM is thin. Only melatonin (in people whose REM is abnormally low) and ketone esters (after very hard training) moved REM in sleep-lab studies. Treat any "boosts REM" claim as unproven unless it cites REM on a sleep study (PSG).

Raises REM

REM is surprisingly stable across age, sex and genes. The clear sex and age differences are in deep sleep, not REM.

No clear effect

Age

~0.6 pts/decade, or none with modern scoring

Evidence: Strong

What this means

REM changes little with age. Total sleep and sleep efficiency carry most of the age decline.

Mechanism

Ageing mostly erodes deep sleep and sleep efficiency. REM, driven by brainstem circuits and the circadian clock, is comparatively well preserved.

Dose / context

REM% fell about 0.6 points per decade through most of adulthood, levelling off in the mid-70s; a 2019 meta with modern scoring found no significant change (0.0 points per decade). Floyd 2007 (382 studies, n=4,171); Boulos 2019 (169 studies, n=5,273).

Source

Floyd et al., Sleep 2007

No clear effect

Sex / Hormones

REM% didn't vary with sex across 169 PSG studies

Evidence: Strong

What this means

REM is about the same in men and women. Deep sleep is where the sexes differ.

Mechanism

Sex differences show up in deep sleep, which is higher in women. Hormonal shifts within a person (menstrual phase, pregnancy) move REM more than sex itself.

Dose / context

REM% did not vary with the share of men across 169 PSG studies; the clear sex gap is deep sleep, which is higher in women. Boulos 2019 (n=5,273); Redline 2004 (SHHS, n=2,685).

Source

Boulos et al., Lancet Respir Med 2019

Lowers REM

Menstrual Cycle (Luteal)

Minor REM dip in the mid-luteal phase

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

REM dips a little in the two weeks before a period, likely because body temperature runs higher.

Mechanism

Progesterone raises core body temperature in the luteal phase, and REM is temperature-sensitive. The dip appears at specific circadian phases.

Dose / context

REM was reduced in the mid-luteal phase at specific circadian phases; reviews call it a minor luteal decrease, linked to higher body temperature. Shechter 2010 (n=8); Baker 1999.

Source

Shechter et al., Sleep 2010

Lowers REM

Late Pregnancy

Fewer REM minutes in third trimester (lab PSG)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

REM tends to be lower late in pregnancy, along with lighter sleep.

Mechanism

Late pregnancy brings physical discomfort, more awakenings, breathing changes and high progesterone, which together lighten sleep and trim REM.

Dose / context

27 women in the third trimester had fewer REM minutes than 24 non-pregnant women on lab PSG; small longitudinal studies are mixed.

Source

Wilson et al., Aust N Z J Obstet Gynaecol 2011

Lowers REM

Obesity (BMI 30+)

7.8 min less REM with apnea excluded (n=1,014)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Extra weight is linked to at most a few minutes less REM once sleep apnea is ruled out.

Mechanism

Much of the link between weight and poor sleep runs through sleep apnea, which fragments REM. With moderate or severe apnea excluded, the remaining REM gap is small.

Dose / context

With moderate or severe sleep apnea excluded, BMI 30+ had 7.8 min less REM (p=0.003); in 2,745 older men, REM was not linked to body composition. Pessoa 2025 (Baependi, n=1,014); Rao 2009 (MrOS).

Source

Pessoa et al., Obesity 2025

Lowers REM

Acute Illness

Evening endotoxin cut REM share (n=15)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Fighting an infection can trim REM for a night, but real colds haven't shown a clear REM drop.

Mechanism

Endotoxin raised body temperature and released immune signalling molecules (cytokines) and stress hormones; non-REM sleep rose and REM fell in step with them.

Dose / context

Endotoxin given in the evening cut REM share in healthy men (P<0.05); the same dose mid-sleep did not; a real cold cut sleep about 23 min with no REM change reported. Pollmächer 1993 (n=15); Haack 2001 (n=10); Drake 2000 (n=21).

Source

Pollmächer et al., Am J Physiol 1993

No clear effect

Genetics

REM heritability h2 0.07 (n.s.) vs deep sleep 0.35

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

How much REM you get looks driven more by circumstances than genes.

Mechanism

In family data, deep sleep was clearly heritable while REM time was not, suggesting REM depends more on circumstances than genes. Some twin studies disagree.

Dose / context

REM time showed no significant heritability (h2 0.07, p=0.19) while deep sleep did (0.35); some twin studies disagree. n=684 family members.

Source

Leocadio-Miguel et al., J Sleep Res 2025

Melatonin

REM% ~14.7% → 17.8% in low-REM patients

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Helps REM only if yours is abnormally low. Otherwise it's a timing aid.

Mechanism

Melatonin acts on the circadian clock, and REM follows the clock. In animals the MT1 receptor selectively enhances REM; in humans the REM gain showed up only in patients who started with reduced REM.

Dose / context

3 mg for 4 weeks raised REM% from about 14.7% to 17.8% (placebo fell to 12.0%) in sleep-disorder patients with low REM; in healthy men REM% did not change. Kunz 2004 (2 RCTs, n=14 total); Rajaratnam 2004 (n=8).

Source

Kunz et al., JCEM 2004

Valerian

REM unchanged after 28 days (n=184)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Valerian doesn't shift REM either way.

Mechanism

Valerian is thought to act on GABA and adenosine signalling. In sleep-lab studies that has not shown up as any change in REM.

Dose / context

REM unchanged after 28 days of valerian-hops in mild insomnia, and in two small PSG studies. Morin 2005 (n=184, industry funded); Schulz 1994; Balderer 1985.

Source

Morin et al., Sleep 2005

Protects REM

Ketone Ester After Hard Exercise

Kept REM at 117 vs 93 min after hard training

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

In trained cyclists, ketone drinks offset the REM loss from a very hard late training day. Untested in everyday life.

Mechanism

Hard training ending an hour before bed cut REM by 26% and nearly doubled time awake overnight. The ketone drink raised dopamine excretion, which the authors offer as a preliminary explanation; the pathway is not confirmed.

Dose / context

A hard training day ending 1 h before bed cut REM from 125 to 93 min; 25 g ketone ester after each session and 30 min before bed (75 g total) kept REM at 117 min (p=0.011 vs placebo). Robberechts 2023 (n=10, crossover); Poffé 2026 (n=24, 7 weeks).

Source

Robberechts et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2023

No clear effect

Glycine (3 g)

Faster sleep onset; REM unchanged (n=11)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Glycine may help you fall asleep faster but doesn't change REM.

Mechanism

Glycine lowers core body temperature and speeds entry into deep sleep, without changing REM latency or stage proportions.

Dose / context

Shortened time to fall asleep and to reach deep sleep; REM latency and stage proportions unchanged. n=11 poor sleepers, crossover; authors from Ajinomoto, a glycine maker.

Source

Yamadera et al., Sleep Biol Rhythms 2007

No clear effect

CBD (300 mg)

REM 17.6% placebo vs 16.6% CBD

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

One dose of CBD didn't change REM.

Mechanism

Unlike THC, CBD does not strongly activate CB1 receptors. A single 300 mg dose in healthy sleepers left sleep architecture, including REM, intact.

Dose / context

REM 17.6% placebo vs 16.6% CBD; REM latency also unchanged. n=26, crossover; authors hold CBD patents.

Source

Linares et al., Front Pharmacol 2018

Evening Intense Exercise

Hard late session: REM −1.95 points (28 trials)

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Hard late workouts trim REM a little and push it later. Morning or moderate sessions don't.

Mechanism

A hard session close to bed likely keeps core temperature, heart rate and sympathetic drive raised into the first part of the night, which delays and trims REM. The studies measured single nights after one session.

Dose / context

One hard session (77%+ HRmax, ending 0.5 to 4 h before bed) cut REM by 1.95 points of total sleep vs no exercise; 2.5 points when it ran over 30 to 60 min. Moderate and light sessions: no significant change. Evening exercise also delayed REM onset by 7.7 min. Morning exercise showed no REM change in timing studies. Yue 2022 (network meta, 28 trials, n=325); Frimpong 2021 (−2.34 points); Stutz 2019 (23 studies).

Source

Yue et al., Nat Sci Sleep 2022

What this means

An ice bath after an evening workout didn't change REM.

Mechanism

Cold water lowers core temperature after exercise, but REM timing and amount did not respond in this study.

Dose / context

No difference in stage proportions or REM onset vs exercise alone. n=11.

Source

Robey et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2013

Alcohol

REM 18 min later, 11.3 min shorter (27-study meta)

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Even a couple of drinks delay REM, and each extra drink cuts more. This is why a nightcap leaves you under-recovered, and why using a drink to fall asleep backfires.

Mechanism

Alcohol's sedative (GABAergic) effect suppresses the cholinergic activity that generates REM early in the night. As it wears off, the rebound arousal and fragmentation hit the second half of the night, where REM concentrates.

Dose / context

REM started 18.0 min later and ran 11.3 min shorter (REM share −2.8 points). Each 1 g/kg added 30.1 min to REM onset and cut 40.4 min of REM. REM delay began around 0.35 g/kg (about 2 drinks), REM loss around 0.5 g/kg (about 3). Meta-analysis of 27 studies in healthy adults.

Source

Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2024

No clear effect

Caffeine

No change in REM% (k=19); deep sleep −11.4 min

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Caffeine doesn't cut REM. It cuts deep sleep.

Mechanism

Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors. Adenosine mainly drives deep-sleep pressure, so caffeine's clearest cost lands on slow-wave sleep, not REM.

Dose / context

No significant change in REM% (k=19), REM minutes (k=17) or REM latency (k=14); deep sleep fell 11.4 min. Meta-analysis.

Source

Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2023

Lowers REM

Nicotine

7 or 14 mg patch cut REM, dose-dependently

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Nicotine cuts REM, and more nicotine cuts more. That held for a patch in non-smokers and for a 24-hour patch in smokers.

Mechanism

Nicotine activates acetylcholine receptors that promote arousal and fragment sleep. A patch keeps nicotine in the blood all night, so the effect covers the REM-heavy early morning.

Dose / context

A 7 or 14 mg patch cut REM time and REM% dose-dependently in non-smokers (n=12); a 24 h patch lowered REM% in smokers (n=15).

Source

Gillin et al., 1994

Mixed

Cannabis / THC

High-dose THC suppresses REM; quitting rebounds it

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Heavy THC can suppress dream sleep, and quitting brings vivid dreams back. At typical doses, the effect on REM is unclear.

Mechanism

THC acts on CB1 receptors and reduced REM eye movements and, less so, REM time in early high-dose studies. Stopping after regular use unmasks a consistent REM rebound.

Dose / context

High-dose THC reduced REM eye movements and, less so, REM time in small early studies; newer lower-dose studies show mixed or no change; REM rebound on withdrawal is consistent. Velzeboer 2025 (meta, 18 PSG studies); Feinberg 1975 (n=7).

Source

Velzeboer et al., Sleep Med Rev 2025

Raises REM

Diet (Carbs)

High-carb raised REM vs low-carb (11 trials)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

More carbs nudges REM up a little. Low carb shifts sleep toward deep sleep instead.

Mechanism

Carbohydrate raises insulin, which helps tryptophan reach the brain for serotonin and melatonin synthesis, one proposed route to more REM. Low-carb diets shift the balance toward slow-wave sleep.

Dose / context

High-carb meals or diets raised REM time and share vs low-carb; low-carb raised deep sleep; REM latency unchanged. Meta-analysis of 11 short-term trials (n=179).

Source

Vlahoyiannis et al., Nutrients 2021

No clear effect

Late Dinner

REM 20.8% vs 20.6% (P=.87), crossover

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Eating dinner an hour before bed didn't reduce REM.

Mechanism

A late dinner raised overnight glucose and cortisol and lowered fat burning in this study, but sleep architecture, including REM, held steady.

Dose / context

REM 20.8% vs 20.6% (P=.87) with dinner at 22:00 vs 18:00 (the 18:00 arm had a 22:00 snack). Crossover, n=20.

Source

Gu et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2020

Lowers REM

Ramadan-Style Daytime Fasting

REM 25.6% → 18.6% during Ramadan (n=8)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Daytime fasting with a pre-dawn meal is linked to less REM, but the 3:30 am wake-up to eat may be the real cause. Both studies were small.

Mechanism

The pattern moves meals into the night and adds a pre-dawn wake-up, which breaks into the REM-rich early morning and shifts circadian cues. The studies cannot separate fasting itself from the schedule change.

Dose / context

REM share 25.6% at baseline vs 18.6% during Ramadan, with sleep times and calories fixed (p<0.05); a separate lab also found less REM. BaHammam 2013 (n=8); Roky 2001 (n=8).

Source

BaHammam et al., Behav Brain Funct 2013

Short Sleep / Early Alarm

4 h vs 9 h: REM minutes cut first (n=27)

Evidence: Strong

What this means

REM is back-loaded, so a short night with a fixed alarm cuts it first.

Mechanism

REM is concentrated in the early morning, so cutting sleep short trims REM more than any other stage, while deep sleep's share actually rises.

Dose / context

4 h vs 9 h for 5 nights cut REM and stage 2 minutes, while deep sleep's share rose. n=27, crossover, PSG.

Source

Shechter et al., Am J Physiol 2012

Lowers REM

Bedroom Heat

32°C room lowered REM vs 22°C; cooling restored it

Evidence: Strong

What this means

A hot, especially humid, bedroom costs you REM. Staying cool protects it, but going colder won't add more.

Mechanism

During REM the body largely suspends temperature control (little effective sweating or shivering), so the sleeper is exposed to the room. Animal studies suggest the brain shortens REM in heat to protect body temperature.

Dose / context

A 32°C bedroom cut total sleep by 24.8 min and lowered REM vs 22°C; a cooling mattress topper restored 21.4 min of sleep and the lost REM. At normal room temperature, cooling added deep sleep, not REM. Pasquier 2025 (n=15, crossover, PSG); Kräuchi 2024 (n=72).

Source

Pasquier et al., J Sleep Res 2025

Lowers REM

Unfamiliar Room (First Night)

Less, later REM on night 1 (53-study meta)

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Your first night in a hotel or new bedroom costs some REM. It recovers by night two.

Mechanism

In a new place the brain likely stays partly on guard, so sleep is lighter and more broken, and REM starts later and runs shorter until the setting feels familiar.

Dose / context

Across 53 studies, the first night in a new sleep setting had less REM and a later REM onset than the second night (p ≤ 0.001); smallest at ages 20 to 29. Meta-analysis, n=1,422.

Source

Ding et al., Sleep Med 2021

Raises REM

REM Deprivation

REM rose to 140% of baseline on recovery night

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Lose REM and your brain pays it back the next night.

Mechanism

REM is homeostatically regulated: a REM debt builds pressure that is repaid with extra REM on recovery nights, scaled to the size of the debt.

Dose / context

3 nights of selective REM deprivation cut REM to 9.2% of baseline; it rose to 140.1% on the first recovery night. n=8 men.

Source

Endo et al., Am J Physiol 1998

Sets timing

Circadian Timing

REM peaks just after the body-temperature low

Evidence: Strong

What this means

Most REM lands in the last hours of the night. Jet lag and shift work misalign it.

Mechanism

REM is gated by the circadian clock. Its propensity peaks just after the core body temperature minimum, on the rising part of the curve, which normally falls in the early morning.

Dose / context

REM propensity peaks just after the body temperature low point, on the rising part of the curve. n=10, 306 subject-days.

Source

Czeisler et al., Science 1980

Lowers REM

Continuous Background Noise

50 dB pink noise cut REM 18.6 min (n=25)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

A constant noise machine or hum can trim REM. Earplugs mostly protect deep sleep, not REM.

Mechanism

Why steady broadband sound cuts REM is not established. The effect is specific: steady pink noise reduced REM, while on-and-off noise peaks mainly cut deep sleep, which earplugs protected.

Dose / context

50 dB pink noise all night cut REM by 18.6 min (118.1 to 99.5 min); 32 dB wind turbine noise cut REM 11.1 min and delayed it 16.8 min. On-and-off traffic noise mainly cut deep sleep instead. Basner 2026 (n=25, randomized crossover); Smith 2020 (n=48); same research group.

Source

Basner et al., Sleep 2026

Lowers REM

Evening Screens

4 h iPad: REM 109 vs 121 min; 90 min phone: no change

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Hours of bright screen time before bed can trim REM. A normal evening on your phone did not.

Mechanism

Hours of bright, blue-rich light in the evening suppress and delay melatonin, pushing the body clock later. REM follows the clock, so a fixed alarm cuts into it.

Dose / context

4 h of iPad reading at full brightness for 5 evenings: REM 109 vs 121 min with a printed book (p=0.03). 90 min of phone reading ending 50 min before bed: REM unchanged (about 19 to 20%). Chang 2015 (n=12, crossover); Höhn 2024 (n=62).

Source

Chang et al., PNAS 2015

Lowers REM

Presleep Stress

Lab stressor lowered REM% and raised REM density

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Stress before bed chops REM up. People whose sleep reacts to stress lose the most.

Mechanism

Stress raises cortisol and arousal into the night. In people with high sleep reactivity, that fragments REM, lowers REM time and loads it with more eye movements.

Dose / context

A lab stressor before bed lowered REM% and raised REM density; REM minutes fell only in people with stress-reactive sleep. n=62, PSG.

Source

Feng et al., Nat Sci Sleep 2023

Lowers REM

Altitude

Higher altitude, less REM (4,559 m vs 490 m)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Sleeping high cuts REM, and higher cuts more.

Mechanism

Low oxygen triggers periodic breathing and frequent arousals, which break up REM and deep sleep. The thinner the air, the bigger the effect.

Dose / context

At 4,559 m, REM and deep sleep share fell across nights 1 to 4 vs 490 m; simulated 2,000 and 4,000 m cut REM share step by step. Nussbaumer-Ochsner 2011 (n=14); Li 2021 (n=25).

Source

Nussbaumer-Ochsner et al., High Alt Med Biol 2011

No clear effect

Daytime Napping

Naps didn't reduce the next night's REM (n=38)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Naps don't eat into your nighttime REM. A late nap may shift when it starts.

Mechanism

Naps spend some sleep pressure, which mostly drives deep sleep. REM follows the circadian clock more than sleep pressure, so nighttime REM amount holds.

Dose / context

Naps from 09:00 to 18:00 did not reduce the next night's REM in young or older adults; an early-evening nap made REM start sooner. Campbell 2005 (n=38); Werth 1996.

Source

Campbell et al., Neurobiol Aging 2005

No clear effect

Phone Radio Signals

Sleep architecture unchanged (double-blind)

Evidence: Moderate

What this means

Phone signals haven't been shown to change REM.

Mechanism

Phone-type radio exposure has nudged some EEG power bands in lab studies, but not the amount or timing of sleep stages.

Dose / context

GSM-type exposure before or during sleep left sleep architecture unchanged (double-blind). Regel 2007 (n=15); Fritzer 2007 (n=10).

Source

Regel et al., J Sleep Res 2007

Lowers REM

Sleeping Sitting Up

Upright seat: 79% less REM than flat (n=6)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Sleeping upright on a plane or in a car cuts REM most. Recline as far as you can.

Mechanism

The authors give two likely reasons: the head is hard to keep comfortable upright, worse in REM when muscle tone is very low, and an upright posture raises sympathetic (fight-or-flight) activity, keeping the body more aroused.

Dose / context

In a 4 h daytime nap, an upright seat gave 79% less REM than a flat seat; a 40° recline gave 37% less. n=6, within-subject.

Source

Roach et al., Chronobiol Int 2018

Lowers REM

Hot Bath Right Before Bed

Bath at 21:00 cut REM; at 17:00 it didn't

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Heating your body hard right before bed pushes REM back. An earlier bath didn't.

Mechanism

Heating the body close to bedtime raised deep sleep and lowered REM. Aspirin, which blunts the temperature response, mostly cancelled the effect, pointing to the late rise in body heat as the driver.

Dose / context

A bath at 21:00 raising core temperature about 2°C cut REM; the same bath at 17:00 changed nothing. 1 h at 41°C ending just before sleep shortened the first REM period. Horne & Shackell 1987 (n=6); Bunnell 1988.

Source

Horne & Shackell, Sleep 1987

Raises REM

Sleeping Next to a Partner

REM 23.0% with partner vs 21.0% alone (n=24)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Sharing a room with your partner was linked to slightly more, less broken REM.

Mechanism

Not established. Couples' sleep stages synchronised when they shared a bed, and people with less social support gained the most REM. The authors propose a feedback loop between REM sleep and social bonds.

Dose / context

REM share 23.0% beside a partner vs 21.0% alone, with fewer REM interruptions (8.5 to 5.4); no other stage changed. n=24 (12 couples), within-subject.

Source

Drews et al., Front Psychiatry 2020

Raises REM

Presleep Learning

Fastest learners had the biggest REM% rise (n=10)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

REM tracks how much you learn. Small study.

Mechanism

REM supports consolidation of procedural and emotional memory. In an immersion course, the rise in REM% tracked how fast students learned the language.

Dose / context

Students who learned French fastest in a 6-week immersion course showed the biggest rises in REM%. n=10.

Source

De Koninck et al., Int J Psychophysiol 1989

No clear effect

Mattress Type

REM unchanged across 4 mattresses (P>0.05)

Evidence: Emerging

What this means

Mattress choice didn't move REM.

Mechanism

How evenly the mattress spread body pressure changed deep sleep and micro-arousals in this study, but not REM. The authors say the underlying mechanism is unknown.

Dose / context

REM unchanged across 4 mattresses (all P>0.05); deep sleep did change. n=16; run by the mattress maker.

Source

Chen et al., PLoS One 2014
