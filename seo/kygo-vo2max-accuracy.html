Weak link

Weak link

Assumed maximum HR (often 220−age)

Not a sensor: an assumption baked into the model. When wrong it directly skews VO2 max, and is a leading error source (especially in highly trained people).

Flagged in INTERLIVE 2022 + Firstbeat methodology

The limiting input is heart rate during the run plus the assumed max HR (often just 220−age). That combination, not the sensor at rest, is the real ceiling on accuracy, which is why a chest strap helps and why very fit people get the biggest errors.

The bottom line

A VO2 max estimate from a hard outdoor run with a chest strap on Garmin / Firstbeat, at about 7% error in general populations, is the most trustworthy. Fitbit / Google is second: the Pixel Watch 3 came in at 8.65% error in a Google-funded study (Lambe 2026). Apple is third, with three independent studies that all show it underestimates (13.2 to 15.8% error; Series 10 MAPE 13.2%, −6.25 mL/kg/min, Lambe 2026). Polar's resting test reads about 1.0 mL/kg/min high against CPET. Samsung, WHOOP, Oura, and Coros rely on company claims with little or no independent peer-reviewed VO2 max validation, and on WHOOP VO2 max accuracy, the company's own data says the error is worst at the extremes.

Verification note. Peer-reviewed VO2 max validation exists only for Garmin, Fitbit / Google, Apple, and Polar; the Pixel Watch 3 study is Google-funded. A frequently-repeated "Galaxy Watch ±4.7 mL/kg/min" figure appears only on marketing/AI-content sites and could not be traced to a real indexed study, so it is deliberately excluded here.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do wearables actually measure VO2 max?

No. The lab gold standard (CPET) analyzes the oxygen and carbon dioxide in your breath. Watches, rings, and straps have no gas analysis, so they estimate VO2 max from heart rate, motion, GPS pace, and your profile (age, sex, weight). Treat it as a fitness trend, not a clinical value.

Which wearable has the most accurate VO2 max?

Garmin (Firstbeat) from a hard outdoor run with a chest strap is the most trustworthy, at about 7 percent error in general populations. Fitbit / Google is second: the Pixel Watch 3 came in at 8.65 percent error in a Google-funded study (Lambe 2026). Apple Watch is third, with three independent studies that all show it underestimates, by 13.2 to 15.8 percent error.

Does the Apple Watch overestimate or underestimate VO2 max?

Three independent studies show the Apple Watch underestimates VO2 max: by 6.07 mL/kg/min (MAPE 13.3%, Lambe 2025), predicting about 41 versus a measured 46 mL/kg/min on the Series 7 (MAPE 15.8%, Caserman 2024), and by 6.25 mL/kg/min on the Series 10 (MAPE 13.2%, Lambe 2026).

Why are resting VO2 max estimates less accurate?

Resting-based estimates, such as the Polar Fitness Test, use resting heart rate plus age, sex and weight with no workout. The INTERLIVE meta-analysis found resting methods as a group overestimate by about +2.17 mL/kg/min on average, versus near-zero bias for exercise-based estimates from an actual run. That is a class average: an independent lab study found Polar’s Fitness Test reads about 1.0 mL/kg/min high, with wide error for any one person.

Do you need a subscription to see your VO2 max?

Most watches show VO2 max for free (Garmin, Apple, Polar, Samsung, Coros, Suunto; Fitbit shows the metric free with Premium adding coaching only). WHOOP requires a paid membership, and Oura requires its membership to see Cardio Capacity.
