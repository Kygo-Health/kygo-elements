Strengths, gaps, subscription and studies

Apple Watch

Counts steps most consistently in daily life, with the most FDA-cleared features (5).

Where it leads

  • Near-zero pooled heart rate bias across 22 studies (Lambe 2026)
  • Best sleep staging in independent testing (κ 0.53, healthy adults)
  • Best wake detection in independent testing (52.2%) and second for REM (68.6%, behind Oura), healthy adults
  • Stays within a few percent on steps over three weeks of daily life
  • Smallest blood oxygen error at normal levels (2.2% in a hypoxia test)
  • 5 FDA-cleared features: ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, AFib History, sleep apnea and hypertension notifications (most of any brand)

Where it falls short

  • VO2 max underestimated in all 3 independent studies (MAPE 13 to 16%)
  • Weight-training calories off by 112% on average (Series 8, Yun 2026)
  • Clinical-sample sleep staging drops to κ=0.30 (Lee 2023)
  • Over-read 85% of blood oxygen readings when true oxygen was below 88% (Series 7)
  • Cleared alerts miss many cases: hypertension 41% sensitivity, sleep apnea 66% (FDA summaries)
  • Hypertension notifications on Series 12 / Ultra 4 on hold in 33 countries (not the US)
  • Series 10, 11 and Ultra 3 have no heart rate or sleep validation

US watches have computed blood oxygen on the paired iPhone since August 2025. Every Apple SpO2 study predates that change or was run outside the US.

Oura Ring

The most accurate for overnight HRV, tied with WHOOP for resting heart rate, and the top sleep staging score in healthy sleepers.

Where it leads

  • Best overnight HRV (CCC 0.99, Ring 4)
  • Top overnight resting heart rate, tied with WHOOP (1.1 bpm off, Ring 4)
  • Highest sleep staging score in healthy sleepers (κ 0.65 vs Apple 0.60, Fitbit 0.55 in Oura's own study)
  • Smallest total-sleep-time bias (-3 min, 6-study meta)
  • Only brand with a published temperature sensor test (its own)
  • Lightest Oura yet: Ring 5 weighs 2 to 2.6 g (Oura spec)

Where it falls short

  • Overcounted daily steps by about a fifth, with single days off by about half (Gen 2)
  • Daytime heart rate error 15% (Gen 3, Gielen 2026), bottom tier
  • Sleep staging κ=0.35 in a clinical sample; 53% accuracy in sleep-clinic patients
  • Calorie algorithm changed May 2025 and has not been independently tested
  • No independent blood oxygen or respiratory rate validation
  • No FDA-cleared features (Oura IPO filing, Sept 2026); Ring 5 blood pressure is wellness only
  • Ring 5 has zero validation of any kind

Sleep κ=0.65 is from an Oura-funded study of screened healthy sleepers that used the same method as the independent studies. In people with sleep complaints Oura scored κ=0.35 (Lee 2023) and 53% accuracy in sleep-clinic patients (Herberger 2025).

Garmin

The best-validated VO2 max, and top tier for workout heart rate.

Where it leads

  • Most independently validated VO2 max (about 7% error, 3 studies)
  • Under 5% step error in the lab (Vivoactive 4)
  • Tier-1 daytime heart rate (Vivoactive 5, MAPE 6.3%)
  • FDA-cleared ECG app on select watches (2023)
  • Up to 11 days of battery (Vivoactive 5, Garmin spec)

Where it falls short

  • Weakest sleep staging (κ=0.21) and wake detection (27.6%, Vivosmart 4)
  • Largest workout calorie error in a four-brand test (77.6% cycling) and 115% in weight training
  • Blood oxygen read low at rest (Venu 2s) and high at altitude (Fenix 5X)
  • Respiratory rate barely tracked real breathing in hospital patients (r 0.28)
  • 11.5% of ECGs came back inconclusive in the FDA study
  • No flagship since the Fenix 6 has peer-reviewed validation

Every Garmin figure here is Fenix 5X/6, Forerunner 245, Vivosmart 4/5, Vivoactive 4/5 or Venu 2s. Fenix 8, Forerunner 970 and Venu 4 have nothing beyond one 2026 hospital preprint.

WHOOP

Strong overnight, but no independent calorie, step or SpO2 data.

Where it leads

  • Second for deep sleep detection (69.6%, behind Oura), healthy adults
  • Overnight HRV CCC 0.94 and resting HR CCC 0.91 (Dial 2025)
  • FDA-cleared ECG, WHOOP MG only
  • Continuous 24/7 wear, no screen

Where it falls short

  • Daytime heart rate MAPE 8.5% vs 12-lead ECG, second worst of five (Van Oost 2025)
  • Total-sleep-time bias +24.5 min (Schyvens 2025)
  • No published calorie, step, SpO2 or independent VO2 max validation
  • Blood pressure is a wellness estimate, not cleared; FDA dropped its warning letter in June 2026 after WHOOP changed the feature
  • WHOOP 5.0 and MG have no validation; VO2 max is 5.0/MG only

The widely quoted "99.7% heart rate accuracy" comes from one night of sleep on WHOOP 3.0 (Miller 2022), in a lab that receives WHOOP support.

Fitbit

The Charge 6 topped a ten-device workout heart rate test.

Where it leads

  • Most population-robust sleep staging: κ=0.42 healthy and clinical
  • Best daytime heart rate in a ten-device test (Charge 6, MAPE 5.5%)
  • Under 4% step error on a treadmill (Inspire 2)
  • Daily calories 9 to 13% off doubly labelled water, the gold standard (Alta HR, Shook 2022)
  • FDA-cleared ECG (Sense, Sense 2, Charge 5 and 6) and irregular rhythm alerts on most models

Where it falls short

  • Worst device in the same ten-device heart rate test was also a Fitbit (Inspire 3, 16.5%)
  • Overcounts steps by 18% in free living (Sense)
  • Smallest average calorie bias, but large misses on single workouts and implausible readings in one study
  • Pooled heart rate under-reads by 3.4 bpm with wide limits (Chevance 2022)
  • Blood oxygen over-reads by about 2 points in COPD (Charge 4, ICC 0.32)
  • No sleep or step validation for any model newer than the Sense / Inspire 2

Since 19 May 2026 Google derives Fitbit VO2 max only from outdoor GPS runs. That method has no published validation.

Samsung Galaxy Watch

Near-ECG heart rate in a heart-patient treadmill test, and little else independent.

Where it leads

  • Near-ECG heart rate on a hospital treadmill test in heart patients (Galaxy Watch 4, ICC 0.997, Kim 2023); not in the ten-device daytime test
  • FDA-cleared ECG, irregular rhythm alerts and sleep apnea risk check (first sleep apnea feature of its kind, De Novo 2024; v2.0 cleared July 2026 for Galaxy Ring too)

Where it falls short

  • Heart rate agreement drops above 160 bpm
  • Blood oxygen read low in an independent hypoxia test (Galaxy Watch 5, bias -2.6)
  • Respiratory rate validation was Samsung-funded and tested a research algorithm, not the consumer feature
  • US blood pressure is a wellness feature, not FDA-cleared, and needs cuff calibration every 28 days
  • No independent VO2 max, step or sleep validation for any current model; Galaxy Watch 8 and Galaxy Ring have nothing

FAQ

Common questions.

Which wearable is the most accurate overall?

No single wearable is most accurate across all metrics. Oura Ring 4 leads for overnight HRV and shares the lead with WHOOP on resting heart rate (Dial 2025). Oura Ring had the highest sleep staging score in healthy sleepers (kappa 0.65 in an Oura-funded study, against 0.60 for Apple Watch in the same study), Apple Watch led independent testing (0.53), Fitbit and Samsung held up best in people with sleep complaints (0.42), Apple Watch has the most consistent step counts in daily life the smallest blood oxygen error and the most FDA-cleared features (five). Fitbit Charge 6 and Garmin Vivoactive 5 were the top tier for daytime heart rate in a ten-device test (5.5% and 6.3% error, Gielen 2026), and Garmin has the most independently validated VO2 max at about 7% error.

How accurate is Oura Ring for sleep tracking?

It depends on who is wearing it. In an Oura-funded study of screened healthy sleepers (Robbins 2024, n=35) the Gen 3 ring reached a kappa of 0.65 against a lab sleep study. In independent clinical samples it falls to 0.35 (Lee 2023) and 53% stage accuracy in sleep-clinic patients (Herberger 2025). Oura is strongest at total sleep time, with a bias of about -3 minutes. Oura Ring 5 has no published validation.

Is Apple Watch or Garmin more accurate for heart rate?

Model matters more than brand. In a ten-device test against a chest strap (Gielen 2026), the Garmin Vivoactive 5 came in at 6.3% error and the Apple Watch SE at 7.3%, with the Fitbit Charge 6 best at 5.5% and the Fitbit Inspire 3 worst at 16.5%. Across 22 studies, Apple Watch pooled bias is close to zero (-0.27 bpm, Lambe 2026). Irregular arm movement, not intensity, is what breaks wrist readings.

Which smartwatch has the most accurate blood oxygen (SpO2)?

Apple Watch has the most evidence and the smallest errors at normal levels: 2.2% in a low-oxygen lab test (Series 8, Walzel 2023) and 2.2% at rest (Series 7, Jiang 2023). Galaxy Watch 5 measured 3.8% and read low; Garmin Venu 2s measured 5.8% and read low. When oxygen is truly low, watches tend to read high: an Apple Watch Series 7 over-read 85% of readings below 88% against arterial blood (Jiang 2026). Oura and WHOOP have no independent SpO2 validation, and no wearable SpO2 feature is FDA-cleared.

How accurate are wearable calorie counts?

Calories are the least accurate wearable metric. In a 2026 study that tested Apple, Fitbit, Garmin and Samsung on the same 58 people against lab gas analysis, cycling errors ran from 31.7% to 77.6% (Kostrna 2026). Apple, Garmin and Samsung read high on average; Fitbit averaged close to zero, but only after implausible readings were removed. In weight training, Apple, Garmin and Samsung errors ran from 84.5% to 115% (Yun 2026), and a second 2026 study of 62 men found all four brands reading high in both endurance and weight sessions (Lee 2026). No study has tested WHOOP calories or Oura's current calorie algorithm.

Which wearables have FDA-cleared features?

Apple Watch has five FDA-cleared features (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, AFib History, sleep apnea and hypertension notifications). Samsung has three (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, sleep apnea). Fitbit has two (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts), Garmin and WHOOP MG one each (ECG). Oura has none. Blood pressure on Samsung, WHOOP, Oura and Pixel Watch is a wellness feature, not FDA-cleared, and no wearable blood oxygen feature is cleared.
