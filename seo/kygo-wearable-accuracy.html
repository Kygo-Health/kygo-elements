Strengths, gaps, subscription and studies

Apple Watch

The most accurate for workout heart rate and blood oxygen, second for steps and sleep stages, with the most FDA-cleared features (5).

Where it leads

  • Most accurate workout heart rate: lowest error of four watches cycling (2.5 bpm, Series 8) and near-zero pooled bias across 22 studies (Lambe 2026)
  • Most accurate blood oxygen: Arms 2.2 in a low-oxygen test (Series 8) and 2.4 against arterial blood in 112 patients
  • Second for sleep stages (κ 0.60) with the best REM detection of any brand (83%), healthy adults
  • Second for steps: +7% in daily life pooled across 59 studies, 6.4% in a 36-adult study
  • 5 FDA-cleared features: ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, AFib History, sleep apnea and hypertension notifications (most of any brand)

Where it falls short

  • Third for sleep timing: misses about half of the time you lie awake (52%) and reads total sleep 8 min high
  • VO2 max underestimated in all 3 independent studies (MAPE 13 to 16%), third behind Garmin and Fitbit
  • Weight-training calories off by 112% on average (Series 8, Yun 2026)
  • Clinical-sample sleep staging drops to κ=0.30 (Lee 2023)
  • Over-read 85% of blood oxygen readings when true oxygen was below 88% (Series 7)
  • Cleared alerts miss many cases: hypertension 41% sensitivity, sleep apnea 66% (FDA summaries)
  • Hypertension notifications on Series 12 / Ultra 4 on hold in 33 countries (not the US)
  • Series 10, 11 and Ultra 3 have no heart rate or sleep validation

US watches have computed blood oxygen on the paired iPhone since August 2025. Every Apple SpO2 study predates that change or was run outside the US.

Oura Ring

The most accurate for everything measured overnight: sleep stages, sleep timing, HRV and resting heart rate. Nothing to show in the daytime.

Where it leads

  • Most accurate overnight HRV: 6% error, agreement 0.99 (Ring 4, 139 nights, independent)
  • Most accurate resting heart rate overnight: 1.1 bpm, 1.9% (Ring 4; Polar 2.71% and WHOOP 1.8 bpm in the same study)
  • Most accurate sleep stages in healthy sleepers (κ 0.65 vs Apple 0.60, Fitbit 0.55 in Oura's own study; independent preprint agrees on the order)
  • Most accurate sleep timing: total sleep within 2 min, catches 73% of wake (96 adults, multi-night)
  • Only brand with a published temperature sensor test (its own)
  • Lightest Oura yet: Ring 5 weighs 2 to 2.6 g (Oura spec)

Where it falls short

  • Steps 50 to 69% off before its May 2025 algorithm change; nothing published since
  • Daytime heart rate error 15% (Gen 3, Gielen 2026), bottom tier
  • Sleep staging κ=0.35 in a clinical sample; 53% accuracy in sleep-clinic patients
  • Calorie algorithm changed May 2025 and has not been independently tested
  • No independent blood oxygen or respiratory rate validation
  • No FDA-cleared features (Oura IPO filing, Sept 2026); Ring 5 blood pressure is wellness only
  • Ring 5 has zero validation of any kind

Sleep κ=0.65 is from an Oura-funded study of screened healthy sleepers that used the same method as the independent studies. In people with sleep complaints Oura scored κ=0.35 (Lee 2023) and 53% accuracy in sleep-clinic patients (Herberger 2025).

Garmin

The most accurate for steps and VO2 max (with a chest strap), second for workout heart rate.

Where it leads

  • Most accurate steps: +3% in daily life pooled across 59 studies, 1 to 2% on a treadmill in 258 adults
  • Most accurate VO2 max with a chest strap: 6.7% error in 35 runners, about 7% in two more lab tests
  • Second for workout heart rate: 2.9 bpm cycling, 6.3% in a ten-device test (Vivoactive 5)
  • Third for overnight HRV (10.5% error, CCC 0.87, Fenix 6)
  • FDA-cleared ECG app on select watches (2023)
  • Up to 11 days of battery (Vivoactive 5, Garmin spec)

Where it falls short

  • Weakest sleep: stages κ 0.21 to 0.25, catches 27% of wake, reads total sleep 44 min high
  • Wrist-only VO2 max read 13 mL/kg/min high in 207 mostly older women (56% error)
  • Largest workout calorie error in a four-brand test (77.6% cycling) and 115% in weight training
  • Blood oxygen read low at rest (Venu 2s) and high at altitude (Fenix 5X)
  • Respiratory rate barely tracked real breathing in hospital patients (r 0.28)
  • 11.5% of ECGs came back inconclusive in the FDA study
  • No flagship since the Fenix 6 has peer-reviewed validation

Every Garmin figure here is Fenix 5X/6, Forerunner 245, Vivosmart 4/5, Vivoactive 4/5 or Venu 2s. Fenix 8, Forerunner 970 and Venu 4 have nothing beyond one 2026 hospital preprint.

WHOOP

Second for HRV and breathing rate, third for resting heart rate. No peer-reviewed test for anything in the daytime.

Where it leads

  • Second for overnight HRV: 8% error, agreement 0.94 (4.0, 289 nights, independent)
  • Third for resting heart rate overnight: 1.8 bpm, 3.0% (same study), 0.3 points behind Polar
  • Second for breathing rate: +0.1 breaths/min vs sleep-lab belts (Strap 2.0, WHOOP-funded, peer reviewed)
  • Caught 70% of deep sleep in a mixed sample (Schyvens 2025)
  • FDA-cleared ECG, WHOOP MG only
  • Continuous 24/7 wear, no screen

Where it falls short

  • Daytime heart rate MAPE 8.5% vs 12-lead ECG, second worst of five (Van Oost 2025)
  • Total-sleep-time bias +24.5 min (Schyvens 2025)
  • No published calorie, step, SpO2 or independent VO2 max validation
  • Blood pressure is a wellness estimate, not cleared; FDA dropped its warning letter in June 2026 after WHOOP changed the feature
  • WHOOP 5.0 and MG have no validation; VO2 max is 5.0/MG only

The widely quoted "99.7% heart rate accuracy" comes from one night of sleep on WHOOP 3.0 (Miller 2022), in a lab that receives WHOOP support.

Where it leads

FAQ

Common questions.

Which wearable is the most accurate overall?

No single wearable is most accurate across all metrics. Oura Ring is the most accurate for everything measured overnight: sleep stages (kappa 0.65, Ring 3), sleep timing (total sleep within 2 minutes of a lab study), overnight HRV (6% error, agreement 0.99, Ring 4) and resting heart rate (1.1 bpm). Apple Watch is the most accurate for heart rate during exercise (lowest error of four watches cycling, 2.5 bpm) and blood oxygen (2.2 points off in a low-oxygen test), and has the most FDA-cleared features (five). Garmin is the most accurate for steps (3% over in daily life, pooled across 59 studies) and VO2 max (6.7% error with a chest strap). Fitbit is the least bad for calories and the only brand with an independent breathing rate test. WHOOP is second for HRV and breathing rate and third for resting heart rate. Polar is second for resting heart rate (2.71% error over the first four hours of sleep, Grit X Pro). Samsung is second for blood oxygen and calories. These rankings are for the models tested, mostly one to three generations old; the newest models have little or no published validation.

How accurate is Oura Ring for sleep tracking?

It depends on who is wearing it. In an Oura-funded study of screened healthy sleepers (Robbins 2024, n=35) the Gen 3 ring reached a kappa of 0.65 against a lab sleep study. In independent clinical samples it falls to 0.35 (Lee 2023) and 53% stage accuracy in sleep-clinic patients (Herberger 2025). Oura is strongest at total sleep time, with a bias of about -3 minutes. Oura Ring 5 has no published validation.

Is Apple Watch or Garmin more accurate for heart rate?

Apple, by a small margin. In the one cycling test that put both on the same people (Kostrna 2026) the Apple Watch Series 8 had the lowest error of four watches (2.5 bpm) and the Garmin Forerunner 955 was second (2.9 bpm); across 22 studies Apple's pooled bias is close to zero (-0.27 bpm, Lambe 2026). In a ten-device test against a chest strap (Gielen 2026) the Garmin Vivoactive 5 came in at 6.3% and the budget Apple Watch SE at 7.3%, with the Fitbit Charge 6 best at 5.5% and the Fitbit Inspire 3 worst at 16.5%. Model matters as much as brand, and irregular arm movement, not intensity, is what breaks wrist readings.

Which smartwatch has the most accurate blood oxygen (SpO2)?

Apple Watch has the most evidence and the smallest errors at normal levels: 2.2% in a low-oxygen lab test (Series 8, Walzel 2023) and 2.2% at rest (Series 7, Jiang 2023). Galaxy Watch 5 measured 3.8% and read low; Garmin Venu 2s measured 5.8% and read low. When oxygen is truly low, watches tend to read high: an Apple Watch Series 7 over-read 85% of readings below 88% against arterial blood (Jiang 2026). Oura and WHOOP have no independent SpO2 validation, Polar produced no readings, and no wearable SpO2 feature is FDA-cleared.

How accurate are wearable calorie counts?

Calories are the least accurate wearable metric. In a 2026 study that tested Apple, Fitbit, Garmin and Samsung on the same 58 people against lab gas analysis, cycling errors ran from 31.7% to 77.6% (Kostrna 2026). Apple, Garmin and Samsung read high on average; Fitbit averaged close to zero, but only after implausible readings were removed. In weight training, Apple, Garmin and Samsung errors ran from 84.5% to 115% (Yun 2026), and a second 2026 study of 62 men found all four brands reading high in both endurance and weight sessions (Lee 2026). No study has tested WHOOP calories or Oura's current calorie algorithm.

Which wearables have FDA-cleared features?

Apple Watch has five FDA-cleared features (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, AFib History, sleep apnea and hypertension notifications). Samsung has three (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts, sleep apnea). Fitbit has two (ECG, irregular rhythm alerts), Garmin and WHOOP MG one each (ECG). Oura has none. Blood pressure on Samsung, WHOOP, Oura and Pixel Watch is a wellness feature, not FDA-cleared, and no wearable blood oxygen feature is cleared.
